# Open Volume — Photography & Art Direction Execution v1

This document converts the approved art-direction guardrails into a production-ready shot system.

## The central rule

**Open Volume does not own one visual world. It owns a way of framing many worlds.**

The recurring identity comes from:
- taste
- scale
- composition
- negative space
- human presence
- authored light
- typography
- selective brand signatures

It does **not** come from repeating one geography, one architectural language, one CGI aesthetic or one lighting trick.

## Launch visual territory

The initial audience is EDM-adjacent, so the first image library must visibly understand electronic music culture without trapping Open Volume inside one version of it.

The launch library should contain all of the following territories.

### 01 / Nightclub intimacy
Real room density, DJ booth proximity, haze, practicals, silhouettes, audience faces, hands, sweat, low ceilings, reflections, imperfect movement.

Avoid arena lighting disguised as a club.

### 02 / Festival scale
Crowd mass, arrival, horizon, stage relationship, artist-to-audience energy, site context.

Drone imagery should establish geography and scale, not simply prove that a drone was used.

### 03 / Artist presence
DJ, vocalist, producer, musician or collaborator shown as an active creative subject rather than anonymous talent inside spectacle.

Prioritize:
- concentration
- gesture
- eye line
- physical performance
- preparation
- artist/audience exchange

### 04 / Process
Lighting programmers, directors, camera teams, rehearsal, soundcheck, visual systems, production conversations and tactile details.

Capability should be visible without turning Open Volume into a production-services brand.

### 05 / Place
Contemporary architecture, industrial space, urban night, cultural venues, coast/water, lush nature, minimal studios, destination environments and other location vocabularies.

Do not over-index toward caves, deserts, carved stone or mountain imagery.

### 06 / Constructed / synthetic environments
Use only when the scene has a credible musical and spatial premise.

A constructed environment should feel photographable.

Avoid:
- generic portals
- floating planets
- neon cyberspace
- anonymous chrome futurism
- glowing spheres
- “AI world” shorthand

## Shot matrix for the first 30-image library

Target mix:

| Category | Count | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Nightclub / intimate performance | 6 | Cultural credibility + presence |
| Festival / large-scale live | 4 | Scale + audience desire |
| Artist close / medium | 5 | Artist value + human detail |
| Audience / shared experience | 4 | Presence + relationship |
| Process / backstage | 4 | Capability |
| Place / architecture / destination | 4 | Breadth |
| Constructed / synthetic | 3 | Future-facing range without dominance |

## Camera-language mix

Across those 30 frames:

- 3–4 drone / aerial
- 5 architectural or environmental wides
- 7 medium observational
- 6 intimate close-ups
- 3 crowd POV
- 2 performer POV
- 3 backstage / process
- several detail inserts distributed across the above

No single lens language should dominate the brand.

## Lighting system

Look for:
- darkness with deliberate information
- controlled practicals
- pools of light
- silhouettes
- projection
- reflective surfaces
- volumetric atmosphere
- directional contrast
- architectural illumination
- selective saturated color when it comes from the scene

Avoid:
- indiscriminate neon
- generic blue/magenta “EDM” grading
- constant lens flare
- glowing brand graphics
- overprocessed skin

## Brand presence in environment

When Open Volume branding appears inside a scene, it should feel physically plausible:
- LED wall
- projection
- stage fascia
- venue screen
- wayfinding
- environmental graphic
- subtle Resonance signature

Never:
- floating logo in empty space
- giant logo pasted onto every image
- repeated Resonance Aperture framing

## Resonance Aperture use

Use in roughly 10–20% of compositions at most.

Good uses:
- reveal
- threshold
- transition
- spatial emphasis
- occasional campaign hero

Bad uses:
- every carousel cover
- every photograph
- every social post
- a default mask that makes all content look alike

## First image-production test

Before producing the full library, create a 12-frame proof set:

1. intimate nightclub DJ / crowd
2. intimate vocalist / electronic performance
3. producer / collaborator process moment
4. crowd anticipation before a musical peak
5. festival aerial / arrival
6. contemporary architectural venue
7. industrial or urban-night performance environment
8. coastal / water-led destination environment
9. lush / organic setting
10. minimal studio / rehearsal
11. authored synthetic environment with no sci-fi shorthand
12. backstage lighting / camera / production craft

### Approval test

Approve the proof set only if:
- all 12 images could plausibly belong to the same **brand**
- they do **not** look like the same **world**
- artists and audiences remain culturally credible
- nightclub frames read as nightclub culture
- synthetic imagery does not dominate
- Open Volume feels premium without becoming sterile
- no frame relies on generic “future” symbolism
