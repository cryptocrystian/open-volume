# Open Volume — Photography & Art Direction Brief / Working v1

## Strategic requirement
Open Volume must not inherit the visual identity of any one world, venue, geography, climate, or architectural motif. The brand owns a **way of framing many worlds**, not a single recurring environment.

The imagery must communicate stakeholder value:
- **Fans:** desire, discovery, energy, presence, and moments worth remembering.
- **Artists:** creative elevation, respect for individual identity, and premium production.
- **Partners / sponsors:** cultural relevance, intentional integration, and premium media value.
- **Venues / destinations:** differentiated programming, place-led storytelling, and production capability.
- **Creative collaborators:** a serious canvas for ambitious work.

## Launch-community context
The initial community is EDM, so the image system must understand real club, festival, DJ, vocalist, producer, and electronic-performance culture while remaining broad enough for Open Volume's later expansion.

## Required subject mix
- DJs performing and preparing.
- EDM vocalists and live collaborators.
- Artist / audience interaction.
- Real nightclub culture: intimate rooms, crowd density, booths, haze, practical light, movement, architecture.
- Festival scale and audience energy.
- Rehearsal, backstage, production, creative direction, lighting, camera, and technical craft.
- Venues, destinations, architecture, city environments, natural settings, and unexpected spaces.
- Visible but tasteful Open Volume presence in venue screens, signage, projection, stage architecture, environmental graphics, or production details.

## Shot-language diversity
Use a deliberate mix of:
- drone / aerial
- monumental architectural wide
- medium observational
- intimate close-up
- backstage / process
- crowd POV
- performer POV
- environmental detail
- movement and long-lens compression where appropriate

Avoid allowing one camera language to become synonymous with the brand.

## Lighting
Lighting should feel authored, not generic:
- pools of light
- silhouette
- reflections
- projection
- volumetric atmosphere
- practical sources
- architectural illumination
- controlled color contrast
- shadow as composition

Nightclub imagery must read as nightclub culture rather than generic arena/concert beams.

## Synthetic / virtual imagery
Use with restraint. Avoid default sci-fi shorthand unless a specific production requires it:
- glowing planets
- generic cosmic portals
- floating spheres
- neon cyberspace
- chrome futurism
- anonymous AI-world imagery
- spectacle with no musical or cultural premise

A virtual environment should feel like a place worth photographing, not proof that the team can render CGI.

## Resonance Aperture
The Resonance Aperture is a **secondary proprietary device**, not a default frame.
- Use selectively for entry, reveal, spatial emphasis, or transition.
- Prefer roughly 10–20% of branded compositions rather than ubiquitous use.
- The more prominent the aperture, the quieter the rest of the composition.
- The environment behind it should keep changing.
- Never let it become shorthand for caves, deserts, canyons, or one world aesthetic.

## Core test
Every production image should answer at least one of these questions:
- Does this create desire?
- Does this demonstrate taste?
- Does this reveal real capability?
- Does this strengthen a stakeholder relationship?

Anything generic enough to belong to any premium-music brand should be reconsidered.
